We had the chance to contribute with an Audio-Visual Installation that took place at the now recognized UNESCO site of Fort Jesus, Mombasa (Kenya). The fort today stands as a symbol of dialogue between cultures and an exceptional stage for a project that gave us the chance to blend together contemporary art, historical memory, and technological innovation. 

Commissioned by the Italian Institute of Culture in Nairobi and the Embassy of Italy in Kenya, we created a video mapping on the walls of the Fort, combining music, imagery, architecture, and research into an immersive experience capable of enhancing the intrinsic nature of the space, amplifying its history and identity. 

Alongside the Kenyan musician Juma Tutu, we also had the honor to present a live performance combining music and video mapping. Juma Tutu, leader of the Swahili Jazz band, whose music is deeply rooted in the Swahili culture of the East African coast, has been a key figure in establishing a bridge to the sonic image of Mombasa. Juma guided us through a process of listening, understanding, and reimagining local musical traditions. 

For the music we took inspiration from the instrumentation used in the coastal region, principally the *Kayamba* and the *Ndema*. A strong influence in fact comes from the rhythms that the more ethnic music builds upon, from which the first part of the piece builds upon. String instruments like the *Nyatiti* and the *Burkandit* also have been of influence. Through the use of a timbral transfer technique, field recordings of Mombasa have been used to create sonic textures recalling the instruments.

The influence that comes from the Mijikenda music tradition, is blended with the influence of the minimalism of the 60s of composers like Steve Reich and Philip Glass. In the rhythmical part in fact marimbas play a crucial role in the creation of the pattern recalling on the sonorities from both aspects.

Curated by Isabella Indolfi, the project builds upon site-specific research already explored in contexts like the 2025 Seminaria Sogninterra Public Art Biennale.


The initiative pays tribute to Fort Jesus and the legacy of Giovanni Battista Cairati, the 16th-century Italian architect and military engineer who designed the structure, considered an extraordinary example of Renaissance architecture.
