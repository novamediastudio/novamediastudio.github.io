The event—organized in collaboration with the Embassy of Italy, the National Museums of Kenya (NMK), and the Italian MedOr Foundation—will feature a performance by the duo from Nova Media Studio, Federica Sorba and Eduard Tampu, arriving from Italy, together with musician Juma Tutu.

The evening featured the Nova Media Studio duo of Federica Sorba and Eduard Tampu, alongside musician Juma Tutu, for a performance combining live music and video mapping projected onto the fort’s walls.

The initiative pays tribute to Fort Jesus and the legacy of Giovanni Battista Cairati, the 16th-century Italian architect and military engineer who designed the structure, considered an extraordinary example of Renaissance architecture.

Now recognized as a UNESCO site, Fort Jesus stands as a symbol of dialogue between cultures and an exceptional stage for a project that weaves together contemporary art, historical memory, and technological innovation.
